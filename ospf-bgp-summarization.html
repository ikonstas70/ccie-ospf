OSPF vs. BGP — Summarization & the IGP/BGP Relationship

Both protocols "summarize," but they're not hiding the same thing. BGP summarization reduces how many destinations get advertised. OSPF area summarization reduces how much topology gets exposed. The difference comes straight from what each protocol is actually responsible for.

NLRI — What BGP Actually Advertises

NLRI (Network Layer Reachability Information) is the part of a BGP UPDATE that answers: "what networks am I advertising?" It's distinct from the path attributes, which answer "how do I reach them?"

UPDATE
  Path Attributes:
    AS_PATH:    65001 65002
    NEXT_HOP:   10.1.1.1
    LOCAL_PREF: 100

  NLRI:
    192.168.10.0/24
    192.168.20.0/24
Summarizing the NLRI

Instead of advertising every specific prefix, BGP can advertise one aggregate:

Before 
10.1.0.0/24
10.1.1.0/24
10.1.2.0/24
10.1.3.0/24
After 
aggregate-address 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0 summary-only
NLRI:
 10.1.0.0/22

Benefits: a smaller BGP table, fewer UPDATE messages, less CPU/memory, better scalability, less convergence impact. The summarized NLRI becomes a brand-new route with its own path attributes — it may even carry AGGREGATOR and ATOMIC_AGGREGATE attributes that the individual components never had.

One-line definition: summarizing the NLRI means replacing multiple specific reachable prefixes in a BGP UPDATE with a single aggregate prefix — reducing table size and update overhead while preserving reachability.

OSPF Inter-Area Summarization

An ABR can summarize routes before advertising them into another area:

router ospf 1
 area 1 range 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0

If Area 1 contains 10.1.0.0/24 through 10.1.3.0/24, the ABR generates one Type-3 Summary LSA — 10.1.0.0/22 — into Area 0. From Area 0's perspective, four networks collapsed into one reachability entry.

Here's the subtlety that matters for the exam: inside Area 1, nothing changes. Every internal router still has full Type-1 (Router) and Type-2 (Network) LSA knowledge of the real topology — routers, links, costs. The ABR still knows everything. What's summarized is purely what crosses the area boundary.

So what's actually being summarized in OSPF is reachability information, not topology information. IS-IS and OSPF are sometimes loosely described as "summarizing topology between areas/levels" — more precisely, they hide detailed topology from other areas while advertising only summarized destinations. "Topology hiding" is the more accurate term than "topology summarization."

The Real Difference: Scale vs. Hierarchy
BGP SummarizationOSPF Area Summarization
Primary goalReduce route countContain topology information
What's hiddenDestination prefixesInternal LSDB detail (links, costs, routers)
BenefitSmaller BGP table, fewer updates, less memorySmaller LSDB outside the area, fewer SPF recalcs, failure isolation
Typical scale200,000 customer prefixes → ~100 aggregates (ISP)Area 2 (Miami) link failure → invisible to Area 1 (New York)

Why the difference exists: BGP is not a link-state protocol — it never builds a topology database in the first place, so it has nothing to hide except the destinations themselves. OSPF is link-state; every router in an area holds the full LSDB, so its hierarchy exists specifically to stop that detailed database from leaking across area boundaries and triggering unnecessary SPF runs elsewhere.

Mental model: BGP summarization hides routes. OSPF area summarization hides topology — even though, mechanically, it still advertises a summarized route to do it.

BGP as an Application Over the IGP

This split in what gets summarized traces back to a deeper architectural point: the IGP owns topology and transport; BGP owns reachability and policy.

OSPF/IS-IS answer "how do I get to the next hop?" — routers, links, metrics, shortest paths. BGP answers "which destination should I prefer?" — using Weight, Local Preference, AS_PATH, MED, and communities, not physical shortest path.

          OSPF / IS-IS
              |
              v

PE ---- P ---- P ---- PE
 \                 /
  \_______________/
         BGP

The IGP supplies reachability to loopbacks and transport addresses; BGP rides on top of that. This is a hard dependency, not just convention — one of BGP's fundamental rules is that a route is only usable if its NEXT_HOP is reachable through the existing routing table, which the IGP populated:

*> 10.10.10.0/24
   via 1.1.1.1   ← must be resolvable via OSPF/IS-IS, or the route is unusable

That's the basis for calling BGP "an application running on top of the network": it rides over TCP port 179, doesn't auto-discover neighbors, builds no topology database, runs no SPF, and depends entirely on the IGP for next-hop resolution.

Interview-ready line: the IGP is responsible for transport and topology, while BGP is responsible for reachability and policy. BGP relies on the IGP to resolve next-hop addresses, which is why BGP can be viewed as a routing application running over an existing IP infrastructure.

One-Minute Summary
  • NLRI = the destination prefixes a BGP UPDATE carries; path attributes = how to reach them. Summarization replaces many specific NLRIs with one aggregate.
  • area X range on an ABR generates a single Type-3 LSA for a block of networks — but only across the area boundary. Internal Type-1/2 LSAs inside the area are untouched.
  • BGP summarization hides routes because BGP never had topology to begin with. OSPF summarization hides topology because OSPF's whole LSDB would otherwise leak between areas.
  • IGP = transport + topology. BGP = reachability + policy, dependent on the IGP for next-hop resolution — which is exactly why their summarization goals differ.
